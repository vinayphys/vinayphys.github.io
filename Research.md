# Research  
My work focuses on the physics of soft and disordered materials: glassy liquids, amorphous solids, polymers, and dense active matter, combining large-scale simulations, theory, and collaborations with experiments.


Below is a refined sketch you can use directly in your research page.

---

## Dense Active Matter and Jamming

Short paragraph idea:  
> I study how dense assemblies of self-propelled particles — *dense active matter* — organize, jam, and yield under driving. My work focuses on how activity, persistence, and interactions modify classical jamming scenarios and generate avalanches, intermittent dynamics, and dynamic heterogeneity. This combines large-scale active-matter simulations with theoretical analysis of mechanical and dynamical observables.

**Related articles:**  
1. *Jamming in dense active matter* (ongoing)  
2. *Avalanches in dense active matter* (ongoing)  
3. *Dynamic heterogeneity in active liquids* (ongoing)

## Polymer Viscoelasticity and Non-affine Lattice Dynamics

Short paragraph idea:  
> Using non-affine lattice dynamics and vibrational-mode analysis, I develop atomistic and coarse-grained frameworks to predict viscoelastic properties of glassy polymers across many decades in frequency. This line of work bridges molecular-level dynamics and macroscopic rheology, enabling time-scale bridging in realistic polymer systems and providing quantitative predictions for viscosity and moduli near the glass transition.

**Related articles:**  
1. V. Vaibhav, T. W. Sirk, and A. Zaccone, *Macromolecules* **57**, 10885 (2024).  
2. A. Singh, V. Vaibhav, T. W. Sirk, and A. Zaccone, *J. Chem. Phys.* **162**, 244504 (2025).  
3. A. Singh, V. Vaibhav, et al., arXiv:2511.18406 (2026).

## Thermal Response of Glassy Systems

Short paragraph idea:  
> I investigate how glassy materials respond to imposed temperature gradients and localized heating, focusing on heat transport, structural rearrangements, and mechanical consequences. This includes studying thermophoresis, thermal conductivity, and the coupling between thermal and mechanical fields in glass-forming systems using large-scale molecular dynamics simulations.

**Related articles:**  
1. V. Vaibhav, J. Horbach, and P. Chaudhuri, *Phys. Rev. E* **101**, 022605 (2020).  
2. V. Vaibhav, J. Horbach, and P. Chaudhuri, *Local heating of glassy matter*, under preparation (2026).

## Rheology of Complex Liquids

Short paragraph idea:  
> My work on rheology explores how complex and heterogeneous liquids — including soft glasses and highly polydisperse systems — flow under shear and confinement. I analyze yielding, shear banding, and the interplay between structure and flow, with particular emphasis on how thermalization protocols, boundary conditions, and size dispersity affect macroscopic rheological response.

**Related articles:**  
1. V. Vaibhav and P. Chaudhuri, *Phys. Fluids* **33**, 053103 (2021).  
2. V. Vaibhav, J. Horbach, and P. Chaudhuri, *Soft Matter* **18**, 4427 (2022).  
3. V. Vaibhav, J. Horbach, and P. Chaudhuri, *Phys. Rev. Materials* **7**, 095601 (2023).

## Supercooled Glassy Liquids and Dynamic Heterogeneity

Short paragraph idea:  
> I study the microscopic dynamics of supercooled liquids approaching the glass transition, with a focus on dynamic heterogeneity, transport, and finite-size effects. This includes characterizing non-Gaussian dynamical fluctuations, entropic timescales, and how system size and interaction softness influence diffusion and relaxation in glass-forming mixtures.

**Related articles:**  
1. V. Vaibhav, J. Horbach, and P. Chaudhuri, *J. Chem. Phys.* **156**, 244501 (2022).  
2. V. Vaibhav and S. Dutta, *Phys. Rev. E* **109**, L062102 (2024).  
3. V. Vaibhav, T. Das, and S. Dutta, *Annalen der Physik* **538** (4), e00247 (2026).
