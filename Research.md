# Research  

My work focuses on the physics of soft and disordered materials—glassy liquids, amorphous solids, polymers, and dense active matter—combining large-scale simulations, theory, and collaborations with experiments.

---

## Dense Active Matter: Jamming and Fluidization

I study how dense assemblies of self-propelled particles (athermal active glasses) jam, yield, and fluidize under internally generated forces. Using ultrastable and less-stable amorphous preparations with persistent self-propulsion, I characterize the activity-driven transition from a jammed solid to a fluidized state at a preparation-dependent critical active force. Both arrest and fluidization times diverge near this critical point, and quasistatic increases in activity reveal a softening of the vibrational (Hessian) spectrum, with the minimum eigenvalue vanishing as a square root at yielding—closely paralleling mechanical yielding in amorphous solids. Preparation stability controls the density of soft spots and the heterogeneity of local rearrangements, highlighting how activity-induced fluidization shares critical features with glassy yielding, but is driven internally rather than externally.

## Polymer Viscoelasticity from Non-affine Lattice Dynamics

Using non-affine lattice dynamics and vibrational-mode analysis, I develop atomistic and coarse-grained frameworks to predict viscoelastic properties of glassy polymers across many decades in frequency. This work bridges molecular-scale dynamics and macroscopic rheology, allowing quantitative predictions of viscosity and moduli near the glass transition. Recent developments incorporate non-Markovian memory kernels, yielding a unified atomistic description of polymer viscoelasticity from THz vibrational dynamics down to Hz–mHz mechanical response.

## Thermal Response of Glassy Systems

I investigate how glass-forming and glassy materials respond to imposed temperature gradients and localized heating, focusing on the coupling between heat transport, structural relaxation, and concentration/structural changes. In multicomponent Lennard-Jones glasses, I have analyzed the Soret effect across the supercooled and glassy regimes, showing that interdiffusion and structural relaxation slow dramatically while heat transport remains fast, leading to large concentration gradients, nonlinear Soret response, and history-dependent profiles that can be frozen in by thermal pulses. Building on this, I study front propagation during melting of ultrastable polydisperse soft-sphere glasses under local heating, using bond-breaking correlation functions and thermodynamic profiles to determine how front velocity, interface roughness, and shock-like transients depend on glass stability and melting temperature.

**Related articles:**  
1. V. Vaibhav, J. Horbach, and P. Chaudhuri, *Phys. Rev. E* **101**, 022605 (2020).  
2. V. Vaibhav, J. Horbach, and P. Chaudhuri, *Front propagation during melting of glass via local heating* (in preparation, 2026).

## Rheology and Failure of Glassy and Complex Liquids

I study how glassy and complex liquids yield, flow, and fail under shear and pressure-driven flows. In size-disperse glass-forming mixtures, I analyze bulk rheology and show how macroscopic rigidity arises from a glassy matrix of large particles coexisting with highly mobile small species. I also investigate confined Poiseuille flow, demonstrating how stress gradients, wall roughness, and thermalization protocols (uniform vs wall-driven, with internal temperature gradients) modify local rheology relative to bulk behavior. More recently, I have focused on controlled mechanical failure in glasses, showing how spatial inhomogeneities in local stability—introduced via thermal processing or spatially selective swap-MC annealing—can deterministically set the location and evolution of shear bands and failure pathways.

## Supercooled Glassy Liquids and Dynamic Heterogeneity

I investigate microscopic dynamics and dynamic heterogeneity in supercooled liquids approaching the glass transition, focusing on diffusion, intermittency, and non-Gaussian statistics. In disparate-size mixtures, I show how strong dynamic heterogeneity and collective interdiffusion lead to pronounced finite-size effects in the diffusion of slow species, and how center-of-mass motion must be removed to recover the true localization length and glass transition density. I quantify heterogeneity using information-theoretic measures (negentropy) applied to displacement distributions, demonstrating that entropic timescales extracted from full non-Gaussian statistics differ markedly from conventional moment-based timescales and diverge as intermittency strengthens near the glass transition. More broadly, I explore how persistent non-Gaussian displacement distributions and “Brownian yet non-Gaussian” diffusion emerge in metastable liquids—thermal, driven, and active—revealing universal features of heterogeneous relaxation and rare, extreme events.
